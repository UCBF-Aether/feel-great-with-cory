Read this first — what this page does NOT claim

No head-to-head trial of Feel Great vs a GLP-1 drug was found. So this page does not claim Feel Great works as well as, or is safer than, Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro or Zepbound. It does not call Feel Great "natural Ozempic," and it does not tell you to stop or replace prescribed medication. If you take a prescription drug, talk to your prescriber before changing anything.

What it does do: lay out verifiable, dated facts — format, labelled risks, regain after stopping, prices as reported in 2026 guides, and lawsuits as pending allegations, not findings — so you can compare categories honestly.

Research date: 30 October 2026. Method: web-index search; no primary FDA/JPML/FTC docket was opened live for that research, so most drug/lawsuit figures below are from secondary trackers, not a court docket. This is general information, not legal or medical advice.

1. The injections — who they are

Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus — semaglutide, GLP-1 receptor agonist, Novo Nordisk. Ozempic: type 2 diabetes. Wegovy: chronic weight management. Rybelsus: oral semaglutide.

Mounjaro, Zepbound — tirzepatide, dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, Eli Lilly. Mounjaro: type 2 diabetes. Zepbound: chronic weight management.

Trulicity — dulaglutide, Lilly, type 2 diabetes. Saxenda — liraglutide, Novo Nordisk, daily injection, chronic weight management, older and less potent than semaglutide/tirzepatide in trial comparisons.

Format, in one line: weekly injection (except the oral forms), dose titration over weeks/months, ongoing prescriber monitoring, prescription and insurance barriers. Feel Great, by contrast, is a non-prescription drink system: Unimate + Balance + time-based eating. That is a category difference, not a clinical outcome claim.

2. Severe side effects — as labelled

Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents; human relevance unknown; contraindicated with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN 2.

Other labelled serious risks: acute pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, dehydration-related acute kidney injury, severe gastrointestinal reactions, hypersensitivity, low blood sugar when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas, diabetic-retinopathy complications (semaglutide), and pulmonary aspiration risk during general anaesthesia or deep sedation.

Ileus — intestinal blockage/paralysis — was added to GLP-1 labelling in September 2023 in an FDA-directed label change.

Common effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal pain — most frequent during dose escalation. Tirzepatide trial summaries cite nausea in up to about 45% at 15 mg, diarrhoea 20–30%, constipation 16–29%, vomiting 9–25% (secondary summary; check the trial label for exact dose tables).

Vision loss (NAION) — do not overstate this: the FDA listed NAION as a potential safety signal in its Oct–Dec 2024 quarterly list (published March 2025) and was evaluating regulatory action, while US labels for Ozempic/Wegovy/Rybelsus/Mounjaro still did not mention NAION as of September 2026 in trackers. The European Medicines Agency concluded in June 2025 that NAION is a very rare semaglutide side effect (up to 1 in 10,000) and directed EU label updates. Cite it as a signal under investigation, not as proven causation and not as an established US-labelled risk.

Feel Great side effects, for balance: Unimate contains caffeine (regular is about a cup of premium coffee; Decaf is under 15 mg per serving) and Balance fiber can cause temporary bloating or gas. Fiber and medication timing matters — talk to your provider, and follow the pack label.

3. What happens when you stop — the regain record

Semaglutide, STEP 1 extension: after 68 weeks, mean loss 17.3%. One year after stopping the drug and the lifestyle programme, participants regained 11.6 percentage points — roughly two-thirds of the loss — ending 5.6% below baseline. The share holding at least 5% loss fell from 86.4% to 48.2%.

Tirzepatide, SURMOUNT-4: 36 weeks, mean loss 20.9%. Over the next year, continuers lost a further 5.5% (ending 25.3% below baseline) while switchers to placebo regained 14.0% (ending 9.9% below baseline).

Fairness note, kept in: regain does not prove Feel Great prevents regain — no comparable Feel Great discontinuation trial was found. The safe statement is about GLP-1 durability: weight loss generally requires continued use.

4. Cost and access — as reported in 2026, volatile

List prices commonly quoted at about $1,000–$1,350/month at retail without insurance, depending on product, dose and pharmacy (2026 secondary guides). Manufacturer self-pay prices reported for 2026: LillyDirect Zepbound $299–$449/month by dose; NovoCare Wegovy pens $349–$399/month. Commercial savings cards can bring copays to about $25/month only if the plan covers the drug — they do not help if the plan excludes weight-loss drugs.

Coverage is the bottleneck: a KFF 2025 employer survey, as cited in secondary coverage, found only 19% of firms with 200+ workers covered GLP-1s for weight loss. Feel Great, for comparison, is about $5.30/day on the $159 direct subscription figure from Unicity's materials — check the current price at checkout, prices change.

Compounded copies: the shortage basis ended — tirzepatide shortage resolved 19 December 2024, semaglutide 21 February 2025 — so mass compounding as a cheap substitute lost its shortage basis. The FDA has issued warning letters over "same as / generic" claims and reported dosing errors with compounded semaglutide.

5. Lawsuits — pending allegations, NOT findings

MDL 3094 — GLP-1 gastrointestinal injuries (E.D. Pa., created 2 Feb 2024): PENDING. Personal-injury claims that semaglutide, tirzepatide and dulaglutide caused gastroparesis (stomach paralysis), ileus, bowel obstruction, pancreatitis, gallbladder injury, aspiration and related harms, and that makers failed to warn adequately. Makers deny the allegations. No settlement announced, no verdict, no class certified as of October 2026 trackers. About 4,025 actions pending 1 October 2026 (JPML count via trackers). A general-causation hearing ran 14–18 September 2026.

MDL 3163 — GLP-1 NAION vision loss (E.D. Pa., created December 2025): PENDING. Claims of sudden, usually permanent vision loss (NAION) from GLP-1 drugs. 216 pending 1 September 2026; 247 on 1 October 2026 (tracker counts differ by date). No settlement or verdict reported.

There is NO $2 billion settlement. Widely circulated "$2bn exposure" figures are projections by law firms/trackers, not court findings, settlements or verdicts. Do not repeat them as a settlement.

Bottom line, in plain words: "thousands of lawsuits allege stomach paralysis" is accurate if framed as pending allegations with makers denying them. "Ozempic causes stomach paralysis / was found liable / settled" is false on current sources.

6. Balance vs Metamucil — the closest like-for-like

Balance: pre-meal fiber drink, 4 g soluble fiber per packet as presented, in a patented fiber matrix (US Patent #US20100074969A1), plus Unicity 7X, Bios Cardio Matrix and Bios Vitamin Complex. Part of a system: Unimate + Balance + time-based eating.

Metamucil: psyllium fiber supplement, about 3.4 g per dose in the research summary — check the variant label, doses vary. Its label requires at least 8 oz of fluid and warns of choking if taken dry, and medicines are generally spaced at least 2 hours apart from psyllium. Balance's site publishes no single medication hour-gap — follow the Balance pack label and talk to your provider, do not invent one.

One honest edge for Metamucil, kept in: psyllium has an FDA-authorised heart-disease risk-reduction claim at 7 g/day or more of soluble fiber from psyllium in a low-saturated-fat/cholesterol diet — far above Balance's stated 4 g per serving. Balance does not inherit that claim.

7. Unimate vs coffee — and vs the injections, in one line

Unimate is purified yerba mate, concentrated through Unicity's five-step BioSelect process, with chlorogenic acids, mate saponins and theobromine — presented as balanced energy without the jitteriness and crashes often associated with coffee. Coffee is caffeine, plain and simple, with a crash for many people. Neither is a drug, neither is an injection, and neither is a substitute for prescribed treatment.

Feel Great wins on format and access — no prescription, no injection, no insurance gate, drink system you can track daily — and loses on proven weight-loss magnitude, because that head-to-head trial does not exist.
