Simple habits · Move first

Walk. That's the whole workout.

No gym. No gear. No step-count rule. A daily walk is the simplest habit that goes with the system — and it's the one most people can actually keep.

Why walking fits this system

The system is three drinks and a window. Walking is the third leg that holds it up. Morning Unimate, then out the door. Or a walk after your biggest meal, before the window closes. That's it — the drink, the window, and the walk, every day.

You don't need to get fit first. You don't need to run. You need a pair of shoes and a door.

What a walk actually does for you

Kept plain, and kept honest — this is general wellness, not a promise, and not a cure for anything:

Steadier energy. A lot of people find the afternoon crash gets smaller when they move in the morning instead of sitting through it.

A clearer head. Walking is thinking time. Ideas sort themselves out on a walk in a way they never do staring at a screen.

A better mood. Fresh air and movement beat another hour on the couch, most days, for most people.

Sleep comes easier. A body that moved during the day settles down better at night — and sleep is part of the window, not separate from it.

Legs and joints stay loose. Use them every day and they complain less. Sit all day, every day, and they stiffen up. That's just how bodies work.

None of this is a medical claim. If walking is new for you, or you have a health condition, talk to your provider first — that's the provider note, and it stays on.

The dead-simple starter

10 minutes, every day, at the same time. That's the whole program for week one.

Anchor it to something you already do. The easiest anchor is right after your biggest meal — shoes on, out the door, before you sit back down. Second easiest: right after your morning Unimate.

Build only when it feels easy. When 10 minutes feels like nothing, make it 15. Then 20. Then 30 if you want it. Never jump from zero to an hour and then quit on day three — that's how every failed fitness plan starts.

No counting required. No 10,000-step rule here. Minutes and consistency beat a number on a screen. If you like tracking steps, great — but the habit is the walk, not the counter.

Make it stick

Pair it. Unimate, then shoes. Same order, every day. When the drink reminds you of the walk, you stop having to decide.

Bring someone. Walk with your wife, your kids, your dog, a friend. A walk you share is a walk you don't skip.

Bad weather is not an excuse, it's a detour. Laps around the house, a mall, a big store — movement counts wherever it happens.
